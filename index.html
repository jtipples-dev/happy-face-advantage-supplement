Supplementary Materials: Model Validation and Posterior Predictive Checks

Author

Tipples, J.

Published

May 4, 2026

Overview

This supplement provides comprehensive model validation and posterior predictive checks for all primary analyses reported in the main text. Validation is reported separately for:

  1. Racing Diffusion Models (RDM) - Fitted using brms (Bürkner, 2017) with cmdstanr backend
  2. Ex-Gaussian Models - Fitted using gamlss (Rigby & Stasinopoulos, 2005)

All checks are reported for each of the three experiments (Expt 1, Expt 2, Expt 3).

Racing Diffusion Models

The racing diffusion model (RDM) decomposes decision-making into four latent parameters: correct drift rate (μ), incorrect drift rate (κ), boundary separation (σ), and non-decision time (δ). Model convergence and fit were assessed using multiple diagnostics.

Trace Plots

The trace plots show MCMC chains for each population-level parameter. Good mixing—indicated by overlapping chains without systematic trends—demonstrates successful convergence of the sampling algorithm.

Posterior Densities

Posterior densities are smooth and unimodal, with no evidence of multimodality or boundary effects. Inner (89%) and outer (95%) credible intervals provide uncertainty quantification.

Overall RT Distribution

The overall response time distribution from the model (red) closely matches the observed data (blue), indicating the RDM captures the central tendency and spread of response times.

RT Distribution by Choice Accuracy

Separating by response accuracy (correct vs. incorrect) reveals the model’s ability to capture the faster RTs associated with correct responses. Predicted distributions align well with observed data.

RT Distribution by Expression

The model captures expression-based differences in processing efficiency, with predicted distributions for fearful and happy faces closely mirroring observed data.

Summary Statistics

Observed summary statistics (mean, SD, 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles) fall within the posterior predictive distributions, indicating good model fit across distributional properties.

Accuracy PPC

The model accurately captures the observed accuracy, with the posterior predictive distribution centered near the observed value.

Random Effect SDs

Non-zero standard deviations with credible intervals away from zero indicate meaningful individual differences in model parameters.

Quantile Comparison

The quantile comparison plot shows predicted versus observed quantiles across the response time distribution. Points falling near the diagonal line indicate good model fit across the entire distribution.

Q-Q Plot

The quantile-quantile plot compares theoretical versus sample quantiles. Deviation from the diagonal line would indicate lack of fit; close alignment supports the ex-Gaussian distributional assumption.

Quantile Differences

This plot shows the difference between predicted and observed quantiles. Values near zero across all quantiles indicate unbiased predictions throughout the distribution.

Percent Error

The percent error plot displays relative prediction errors. Small percent errors (typically <5%) across quantiles indicate good relative fit.

Actual vs. Predicted

This overlay plot compares the observed density with predicted densities from the model. Close alignment indicates the ex-Gaussian distribution adequately captures the shape of the RT distribution.

Density Comparison

The density comparison plot provides an alternative visualization of observed versus predicted densities, with good overlap indicating appropriate distributional fit.

Conditional Quantiles

Conditional quantile plots show model fit separately for each subgroup (e.g., by expression, face sex, participant gender). Consistent fit across subgroups indicates the model captures condition effects appropriately.

Summary of Model Validation

Racing Diffusion Models

Across all three experiments, the racing diffusion models demonstrated:

  • Successful convergence: All Rhat values < 1.01, with good effective sample sizes
  • Good posterior properties: Smooth, unimodal distributions without boundary effects
  • Excellent fit to RT distributions: Predicted RTs closely matched observed data overall and by condition
  • Accurate accuracy recovery: Predicted accuracy aligned with observed performance
  • Meaningful individual differences: Random effect SDs indicated substantial participant variability

Ex-Gaussian Models

Across all three experiments, the ex-Gaussian models demonstrated:

  • Good quantile fit: Predicted quantiles closely matched observed quantiles across the distribution
  • Small prediction errors: Percent errors typically below 5%
  • Good density alignment: Observed and predicted densities showed strong overlap
  • Consistent fit across subgroups: Conditional quantile plots showed no systematic biases by condition

Conclusion

Both the racing diffusion models and ex-Gaussian models provide adequate fits to the data across all three experiments. The validation checks support the use of these models for drawing inferences about the effects of expression, face sex, face race, and participant characteristics on response time distributions and decision processes.
